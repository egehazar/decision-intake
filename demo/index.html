How it works

Language in, decisions out, and a number on every doubt.

Each part does only what it is reliable at. The language model never does arithmetic and never decides; the decision engine never parses text.

01 · READ

Typed slots, not guesses

An LLM fills slots: the amount as stated, its period, whether it was approximate, or "don't know". Code converts "about $2,900 a month" into evidence spread over the right yearly ranges.

02 · BELIEVE

A belief over real households

Weights over 48,657 households from PolicyEngine's Populace data, updated through each question's model of how people misreport. A support floor stops it from becoming certain by ruling almost everyone out.

03 · PREDICT

A neural outcome model

A wide-and-deep network, written in plain numpy and calibrated out of fold, learns PolicyEngine's eligibility and dollar amounts for all ten programmes at once. Each candidate household is scored on the person's own answers.

04 · DECIDE

Value of information

Every unasked question is priced by the expected drop in the cost of mistakes across all ten programmes, with one matrix product per turn. It asks while a question is worth its cost, then acts, or hands off when doubt stays high.

Reading people's answers

4B models, fresh amount answers

Asking an LLM to pick a range directly gets one answer in ten confidently wrong. Letting it fill typed slots and doing the arithmetic in code cuts that by a factor of twelve, and the result replicated on fresh answers (D23, D26).

Scope

What the numbers mean.

Rules and people

Eligibility and amounts come from PolicyEngine-US under 2026 rules, assuming everyone takes up what they are entitled to. Households are Populace, a calibrated synthetic population built from census and tax data.

A research system

Not an eligibility determination: a real screener sends people to the official application. Immigration status is never asked, by design, and is reported as a source of error.

How it was tested

Settings frozen on a validation split, then scored once on fresh test households with paired bootstrap intervals. Every decision and every withdrawn result is logged in DECISIONS.md.

A question is worth asking when the mistakes it is expected to prevent outweigh the cost of asking: $15 a year per question. Missing a programme a household can get costs its value; recommending one it can't costs $1,000. Built by AI agents (Claude, reviewed by GPT and Fable) under Ege Hazar's direction.
