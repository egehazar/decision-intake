Caseload triage

For food banks, 211 lines and navigator teams: rank the households you already know a little about by the benefits each is likely missing, and see the one question worth asking each of them first.

1. Your list

One row per household, with a header row. Use an id column with your own reference, never names. Any of these columns help; leave unknowns blank: state (two letters), household_size, children, earned_income (dollars a year), tenure (rent, own or neither), housing_cost (dollars a month), child_under5, senior, disabled, married (yes or no), social_security and other_income (dollars a year), assets (dollars). And already: what the household gets now, separated by spaces (snap, medicaid, tanf, ssi, wic, chip, housing). Those are left out of the ranking, and the federal rules they trigger are shown: a household on SNAP with a child under 5 meets WIC's income test, for one. Being on SNAP also sharpens every other estimate (in a registered test on rows like these, it cut mistakes by about a quarter).

Rows are sent to the DecisionIntake engine 20 at a time to be scored and are not stored or logged. Up to 1,000 rows.

2. Who to call first

Sorted by expected value: for the programmes the engine would recommend from what you know, the chance of qualifying times the typical yearly amount (health coverage counted at its value, not cash). In a development check on 2,000 simulated households known by five fields (scored on the original ten programs), the top 20% of this ranking held 61% of the benefit dollars (73% without health coverage), against 20% for a random order and 53% for largest household first. A ranking aid for outreach, not an eligibility decision.
